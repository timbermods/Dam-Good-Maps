# Map mechanics catalogue

The rules below come from the repository's notes and, since the verification pass, from the game's own code
([VERIFIED.md](VERIFIED.md), Timberborn 1.1.2.4). Design effects and limits are proposals inferred from those rules.
“Deny” means remove an optional opportunity, never remove the colony's only viable opening.
“Early”, “middle” and “late” describe dependencies, not a proven completion day (U04, U09).

Source keys name files and sections. They are not external game references:

- **W**: [water_and_soil.md](../notes/water_and_soil.md), Q1 sources, Q2 flow, Q3 moisture, Q4 plants, Q5 contamination, Q6 special sources, Q7 weather and storage.
- **N**: [navigation_ruins_entities.md](../notes/navigation_ruins_entities.md), numbered sections below.
- **B**: [blocks_and_placement.md](../notes/blocks_and_placement.md), §1 footprints, §2 placement, §3 start, §4 slopes, §5 support and height, §6 loading.
- **F**: [format_1_1.md](../notes/format_1_1.md), §6 entities and §7 initial simulation state.
- **J**: [blueprints_summary.json](../notes/blueprints_summary.json), exact template and spec keys; [footprints.json](../notes/footprints.json), `_meta` and named templates.
- **P**: [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md). Its design targets are not game constants.
- **V**: [VERIFIED.md](VERIFIED.md), the 12 facts checked against the decompiled game, by ID (V U01 … V U12).

## Water, power and hazards

| # / mechanic | Sourced rule and faction difference | Terrain leverage and player decision (design inference) | Emphasise, limit or deny; fairness limit | Existing analysis or new measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01. Flow and routing | W Q1–Q2: source strength is volume per second, water follows head and stored momentum, open edges drain, padding beside emitters is walled. No faction difference established. | Branches, loops, narrow channels, falls and edge outlets decide where water can be caught or redirected. Early: choose a bank; middle: redirect supply. | Vary network and source positions independently of relief. Limit inflow or split it; deny one route while leaving clean pumpable water. Flooding the only start or draining its only intake is an invalid opening. | Existing `metrics.cleanStrength`, river counts and `water.outflow`. New: actual settled flow graph, bottleneck discharge, alternate-route cuts and source-to-intake paths. |
| 02. Water wheels and falls | V U01: power = rated hp × \|mean over blade cells of the net stored outflow along the wheel's axis\|, counting a cell only above 0.15, rounded up. No height or contamination term. Folktails WaterWheel 270 hp over 2 cells; Iron Teeth Compact 120 hp over 1 cell and Large 540 hp over 2. Water above base + 2 blocks a wheel. | A steady, one-directional reach beside buildable bed suggests an industry site; a fall helps only through the flow it makes. Middle: move production toward flow or bring another power source. | Vary local discharge, width, bank room and access. Limit flow or put strong reaches far away. Denying wheel sites is acceptable only if another power source remains; wind exists only for Folktails (V U10). | Existing settled outflows and waterfall clusters. This study now measures `peakAxialFlow64` and the two small wheels' hp beside a bank. New: legal wheel footprints (bed level, 3 clear levels), flood clearance and drought output. |
| 03. Dams, floodgates and levees | W Q2: full and partial obstacles change flow. N §5–§6: Blockage is a full one-level obstacle like a levee; NaturalDam/player Dam crest is 0.65. V U02: 1-, 2- and 3-level floodgates (150/250/500 science), identical for both factions, set in 0.05 steps; nothing passes below a crest. Dam 20 logs, no science; Levee 12 logs, 120 science. W Q3: levees block moisture from above. V U11: closures can be built in flowing water and act tile by tile as they finish. | Narrow cuts and wide basins offer cheap storage; broad low banks require more work. Early/middle: choose a closure, storage height and downstream route. | Vary dam-line length, basin shape and start position. Limit convenient sites. Denying the planned nearby site conflicts with P D25/D30/D58/D85 and awaits decision #31. Never call an unbuildable line a solution. | Existing `damSites`, volume, area, length, crest and leak test. New: actual block count by crest, legal construction access, floodgate operation and inundation masks. |
| 04. Drought | W Q1/Q7: sources stop after a ramp; the BadwaterPressurizer is the stated Iron Teeth exception for its badwater source. Evaporation does not increase with weather. | Pools below spill height persist; a river with large inflow may still empty. Early: keep drinking water; middle: deepen storage and preserve fertile banks. | Vary retained water separately from source strength. Deny a ready pool only with a feasible storage path. A large river is not proof of drought safety. | Existing `droughtStorage` and `water.reservoir`; new pump-accessible clean volume, depletion with consumption, refill time and early-cycle work budget. |
| 05. Badtides | W Q1/Q7: clean sources emit contaminated water; drains activate; aquifer drills stop for the whole hazard (V U05, confirmed). Badtides first occur in cycles 6/5/4 for Easy/Normal/Hard. | A separate reservoir, bypass and high safe bank create a different problem from simply storing more water. Middle: isolate, divert, then flush. | Vary catchments and clean-storage separation. Limit convenient diversions. Never promise a permanently clean flowing surface source in badtide. | Current steady-state contamination is not a badtide test. New: time-varying source contamination, protected intake days, bypass capacity, first threat time and flush delay (U09). |
| 06. Reservoir geometry and evaporation | W Q2/Q7: wide water loses about 0.0535 depth/day; thin water loses more. N §11 and W Q7: basic Folktails pump reaches 2, Iron Teeth deep pump 6; large pump 4 is listed. | Deep compact basins store more per exposed area, but depth beyond pump reach is not immediately usable. Early/middle: choose shore, intake and later access. | Vary surface area, depth and spill sill. Limit storage with shallow shelves. Do not count unreachable deep water as starting supply or treat initial volume as volume left after drought. | Existing analytic retained depth. New: accessible volume curves by water level and faction, shore loss, evaporation-to-usable-volume ratio and clean refill connectivity. |
| 07. Seeps | W Q6: 2×2, off above 0.8 depth and back on below 0.72; stop in drought. WaterSeep emits badwater in badtide. | Shallow local basins can support pockets away from rivers. Early/middle: use a small oasis or connect it to a larger system. | Vary floor height and connection. Limit depth; deny seeps as optional content. Do not count them as deep reservoir fillers or permanent drought water. P §5.7 defers them. | Existing `sim/model` seep hysteresis and `mechanicsOf` flags. New: local accessible storage and event supply profile. |
| 08. Aquifers and drills | W Q6, V U05: aquifer needs a finished powered drill; output scales with the network's power efficiency. Ancient drill takes 200 hp; player drill 400, with 400 science and material costs. No output in any hazard; the drought ramp cuts flow S/2.67 days before a drought; no badwater. B §2 requires matching understructure. | An aquifer near power and an empty basin offers a power-for-water project. Middle: connect an ancient drill or build one. | Pair with an accessible power route; separate the two for a larger project. Deny aquifers freely as optional content. Never make one the only initial supply. P §5.7 leaves them out. | Existing source classification, footprint validation and zero initial aquifer output. New: power connection cost and conditional output/storage; no free water credit. |
| 09. Geothermal fields | N §9: both factions' engine gives 400 hp, no workers or fuel; costs 50 logs, 30 planks, 160 science. Flooding the centre stops it. | A dry field beside a cliff or frontier can pull industry away from the starting bank. Middle: expand for dependable power. | Vary road length, intervening barriers and flood exposure; omit fields to remove this bonus. Do not treat a placed field as an already built engine. | Existing count and `extras.placement`. New: dry footprint access, route to consumers, flood envelope and commissioning dependency. |
| 10. Badwater and contamination | W Q5: soil spread starts at water contamination ≥0.5, reaches about 7 tiles; contaminated soil kills plants. Beaver exposure is to water ≥0.05, not soil. Folktails contamination barriers preserve moisture; Iron Teeth irrigation barriers block both. Dome/rig/pressurizer controls differ as listed there. | Close badwater may force a safer route or protected farm edge; distant badwater delays that trade-off. Middle: contain, bypass or exploit it. | Vary flow path, bank height and crossings, not source distance alone. Deny permanent badwater as a setting, without claiming badtides vanish. A safe opening must not rely on walking through contaminated water. | Existing soil map and `start.badwater`. New: safe-route detour, wet exposure length, contaminated fertile share and barrier cost. Badwater distance remains advisory (D85). |
| 11. Natural dams | N §6: 0.65 crest, not walkable on top, demolished in 4 hours, removal releases water. J `NaturalDam.FinishableWaterObstacleSpec` agrees. | A pre-existing pool can save storage work but block a crossing. Early/middle: preserve the pool or reopen a route. | Vary crest placement and banks. Limit to side channels. Deny freely; never seal the only early route without another way. | Existing model, dam count and walk blockers. New: before/after retained water, walking access and downstream flood footprint. |
| 12. Blockages and release events | N §5, V U08: full one-level plug, demolished in 4 builder-hours that bonuses do not shorten; beavers cross its top at bank level without a path, and adjacent blockages chain. Removal opens water flow. | A plugged spillway offers a choice of when to release stored water or drain land. Middle: clear after preparing downstream. | Vary stored head and reward below. Limit release volume. Deny optional plugs. The release must not destroy the only safe colony route or intake without counterplay. | Existing full obstacle and placement checks. The analysis still blocks the footprint although V U08 allows a bank-level crossing. New: removal scenario, route difference, released volume and receiving basin capacity. |
| 13. Timed sources and badtide drains | W Q1: delayed sources start after their cycle/day condition. W Q6: drains emit only in badtide through a roofed directional notch. | Time changes which valley is wet or dangerous. Middle/late: prepare a bypass before a known opening. | Vary event order and receiving land; keep events outside mandatory early supply. Roofed drains remain out of scope under P D28 and §5.7. | Existing inactive-source classification. New: event graph, earliest-impact route and activation scenarios. Steady state cannot stand in for a timer. |
| 14. Unstable cores | N §8, V U06/U12: not demolishable; a sphere of radius + 1 removes terrain and every object, kills every character in range and sets off neighbouring cores. No direct water or contamination effect. The `UnstableCore` component is required: a map without it crashes on load. | A future breach can connect a reservoir, uncover land or threaten a route. Middle/late: prepare or relocate before the event. | Vary blast geometry and payoff. P §5.4 keeps cores optional, 40+ tiles out, cycle 5+, no chains. Do not use a core to force day-one survival. | Existing count, distance and separation checks. New: blast/terrain-collapse scenario, downstream water, replacement route and post-event resources (U12). |

## What “faction suited” can mean here

Keep separate evidence-backed differences: basic pump reach, listed plant options, contamination barriers,
badwater control, and the mine recipe (W Q4–Q7; N §2/§11). V U01/U10 add power: only Folktails have wind, which ignores
terrain; Folktails have one 270 hp two-cell wheel, Iron Teeth a 120 hp one-cell and a 540 hp two-cell wheel.
Both factions can use geothermal engines (N §9).
Both need the common valid starting footprint (B §3; J `DistrictCenter.Folktails` and `DistrictCenter.IronTeeth`).

Maps should still pass the two-level pump opening rule. A later deep reservoir can offer more intake access
to Iron Teeth without forcing a faction at the start. A low fertile shelf may support the Folktails-only plants
listed in W Q4, but that does not prove a stronger economy. Mangrove habitat is shallow flooded ground;
its recipe value still needs the rest of the food model. Do not place faction-only plants in a shared map (B §2).

Do not invent a single Folktails-versus-Iron-Teeth score. Report the specific opportunity and its evidence.
Wheel power and crop chains are now known (V U01/U03); how fast a colony progresses remains play (V U04/U09/U10).
