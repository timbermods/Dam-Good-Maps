# Landscape families and play

These are sampling families, not generator templates. Pass rates count all three 16-level mappings and all nine windows per centre. They describe this conversion policy, not the fraction of real landscapes that are playable. Nearby windows can miss the named feature. Random land is a control cohort.

| Family | Passed / converted | Library | Formation and process families | Play value and limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| archipelago | 95 / 540 (17.6%) | 2 | Separated land remains above water after flooding, erosion or construction. Inundation of uneven relief, glacial erosion or volcanic growth. | Many routes and island frontiers; playable crossings and retained water need checking. |
| badlands | 237 / 540 (43.9%) | 4 | Weak exposed material develops closely spaced gullies and divides. Runoff incision, slope retreat and episodic deposition. | Many small narrows and vertical barriers; level resource space is limited. |
| braided | 105 / 540 (19.4%) | 5 | Mobile sediment bars divide and rejoin a river's channels. Sediment transport, deposition and channel migration. | Gravel islands and several dam choices; small channels may vanish in a DEM. |
| caldera | 96 / 540 (17.8%) | 5 | Collapse creates a volcanic depression; drainage depends on its rim and breaches. Volcanic collapse, later infill and outlet erosion. | A breached bowl offers a large reservoir and one useful outlet gorge. |
| canyon | 229 / 540 (42.4%) | 5 | Incision cuts into a plateau as uplift and erosion compete. Uplift, drainage incision and differential erosion. | Narrow dams and high frontiers; flat starts can be scarce. |
| coast | 89 / 540 (16.5%) | 3 | Waves and slope failures reshape the boundary between land and sea. Coastal erosion, sediment movement and cliff retreat. | Cliff frontiers and pocket basins; freshwater simulation cannot reproduce the sea. |
| cone | 114 / 540 (21.1%) | 5 | Volcanic deposits build a summit and slopes, later cut by erosion. Layered deposition, crater formation and radial drainage. | Radial frontiers and vertical building; summit relief can consume the height budget. |
| confluence | 194 / 540 (35.9%) | 2 | Tributaries meet a larger drainage route. Drainage capture, branching erosion and sediment exchange. | Competing dam sites and directions outward; a junction can mix hazards and clean water. |
| delta | 19 / 540 (3.5%) | 3 | Sediment accumulates where a river enters slower water. Deposition, channel switching and distributary growth. | Many small barriers and island routes; shallow storage and flooding are risks. |
| escarpment | 143 / 540 (26.5%) | 5 | A steep regional face separates higher and lower terrain. Faulting, differential erosion and scarp retreat. | A strong frontier with a few breaches; water routes can connect several heights. |
| falls | 230 / 540 (42.6%) | 5 | Flow crosses a steep bed step or resistant lip. Differential erosion, retreating knickpoints and hanging tributaries. | Power and height changes; useful storage depends on pools and sills, not the fall alone. |
| fan | 83 / 540 (15.4%) | 4 | Sediment spreads when a confined flow reaches open ground. Deposition, avulsion and declining slope. | Branching dry frontiers and irrigation choices; a surface DEM misses intermittent flow. |
| fjord | 122 / 540 (22.6%) | 5 | A glacial trough becomes a sea inlet. Glacial erosion followed by inundation. | Steep shores and broad storage; a freshwater game conversion loses tides and salinity. |
| glacial | 161 / 540 (29.8%) | 5 | Ice broadens and deepens valleys; tributary valleys may hang above them. Glacial erosion, deposition and postglacial rivers. | Wide floors below high frontiers; hanging tributaries suggest falls and perched water. |
| gorge | 219 / 540 (40.6%) | 5 | A river cuts a narrow passage through resistant ground. Concentrated incision and contrasts in rock strength. | Short barriers and falls; access and room beside water matter. |
| karst | 164 / 540 (30.4%) | 5 | Dissolving rock forms depressions and underground drainage. Dissolution, collapse and subsurface flow. | Separate basins and hidden-water ideas; a heightfield cannot represent their caves. |
| lakes | 112 / 540 (20.7%) | 2 | Basins collect water where erosion, deposition or structure restricts drainage. Basin formation, outlet erosion and connected drainage. | Stored water and chains of basins; shoreline and sill heights control expansion. |
| meander | 118 / 540 (21.9%) | 5 | Outer-bank erosion and inner-bank deposition move bends; cutoffs leave oxbows. Lateral migration, cutoff and floodplain deposition. | Long routes beside short water connections; oxbows can preserve drought water. |
| mesa | 213 / 540 (39.4%) | 5 | Resistant caps survive while surrounding ground erodes. Differential erosion of layered rock. | Raised frontiers and scarce access; channels between remnants offer dam sites. |
| plateau | 160 / 540 (29.6%) | 5 | Broad elevated ground is dissected by drainage. Uplift or volcanic accumulation followed by incision. | Large building areas and branching expansion; deep water may be hard to reach. |
| random | 329 / 1350 (24.4%) | 3 | The land mask supplies a control sample without choosing a named landform. No formation mechanism is assigned from elevation alone. | Shows how strongly named examples and conversion choices shape the survey. |

The badlands row retains the Las Medulas sampling error in its counts. That mining landscape is excluded from natural targets and the library. See [methods](METHODS.md).

Use combinations: a caldera with an eroded outlet gorge; a glacial floor below hanging tributaries; a meandering floodplain beside an escarpment; a confluence between plateau remnants. The terrain must supply the start, water route and dam opportunities. Do not paste a member of this library into a generator.
